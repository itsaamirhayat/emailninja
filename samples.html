Coaching & Info-Marketers Sample 01

The "Accidental Insanity" Price-Drop Flash Sequence

A raw, 3-part text-only sequence, written for a hypothetical elite info-product creator whose course sales have stalled out. Instead of a slick corporate discount announcement, the copy uses the "Reason Why" hook—explaining that a backend system glitch accidentally sent a raw testing coupon to a handful of people, and instead of quietly fixing it, the creator decides to let the next wave of fast-movers grab the same discount.

Why This Works: The "accidental" frame gives urgency a believable cause instead of a manufactured deadline, which is what makes scarcity land instead of feeling like a sales tactic. Sent as plain text from a personal-feeling domain with clean SPF/DKIM alignment, this style is built to bypass the Promotions tab rather than get flagged as a bulk campaign.

RAW SCRIPT — EMAIL 1 OF 3 
Subject: {{ first_name }}, I might get fired for sending you this...

Listen close. I don't have much time before my partner logs 
into the dashboard and sees what I just did. 

If you are reading this, it means you have exactly 12 minutes 
to make a choice that will either save your profit margins 
or leave you at the mercy of Zuckerberg's next ad-cost hike...
E-commerce Stores Sample 02

The "Cart Abandonment Trauma" Flow

Most abandoned cart emails say "Oops, you left something behind!" and offer a 10% coupon. It's boring and predictable. This script takes the opposite approach: a 2-step automated sequence that hits the buyer 45 minutes after abandonment. Step 1 assumes the checkout page glitched on them and apologizes for the technical error, offering a direct link to bypass the line. Step 2 hits 24 hours later with a hard scarcity hook tied to real inventory logic, not a fake countdown.

Why This Works: Apologizing for a "glitch" removes the shame a buyer feels about abandoning, which lowers resistance to clicking back in. Tight, single-sentence paragraphs and high-contrast visual sections keep this scannable on mobile, where most cart-recovery opens actually happen.

RAW SCRIPT — STEP 1 OF 2 
Subject: System error on checkout page (Order #{{ checkout_id }})

Hey {{ first_name }}, it looks like our servers completely melted down 
right when you were hitting the final checkout button. 

I checked the stock log and your cart items are still temporarily reserved, 
but the queue is clearing fast. I opened an isolated personal pipeline link 
for you below so you can bypass the checkout glitch entirely...
Elite Professionals & Newsletters Sample 03

The "Kitchen-Table Dialogue" Long-Cycle Nurture

Written for the scenario of a high-volume newsletter with a large, dormant audience that's stopped opening broadcasts. This sample is a deep-empathy relationship script. It opens with a vulnerable subject line — "I'm deleting half of this list tomorrow morning" — agitates the pain of information overload, and pivots into a raw, one-on-one conversation instead of a broadcast voice.

Why This Works: Threatening to unsubscribe people yourself flips the psychology — instead of the reader ignoring you, they're suddenly afraid of losing access. Plain-text formatting designed to look like a personal note from a friend is what keeps this out of the "Promotions" tab in the first place.

RAW SCRIPT — OPENING NOTE 
Subject: I'm deleting half of this list tomorrow morning.

Let's be completely transparent, {{ first_name }}. 

Your time is rare, and my server bill is getting ridiculously expensive. 
If you aren't opening these messages, it means I am just cluttering your 
inbox space, and I refuse to be another generic corporate spammer...
